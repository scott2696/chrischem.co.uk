How We Review: Our Testing Methodology in Full

By Daniel Fairhurst, Lead Casino Reviewer Updated 02/09/2026

Every score on this site comes from an account we opened with our own money. This page sets out exactly what we test, how each category is weighted, the withdrawal script we run, what disqualifies a site outright, and how we are paid. If you want to check our work, everything you need is here.

In one paragraph

We assessed 38 UK-facing casino sites in 2026 and published seven. Each is scored out of five across five weighted categories: payouts and banking (30%), bonus fairness (25%), games and software (20%), trust and safety (15%) and experience (10%). We deposit real pounds, complete real identity verification, and time real withdrawals with a stopwatch. Operators pay us a share of revenue when a reader opens an account — at an identical commission rate across every brand we list, so position cannot be bought. We criticise every site we recommend, including the number one.

The five categories and their weights

Weights are fixed in advance and applied identically to every operator. We do not adjust them to produce a preferred ranking, and we do not add categories that happen to favour a particular site.

The test protocol, step by step

  1. Pre-registration checks. Licence badge clicked and verified on the regulator's register. Company name and registered address located. Full terms and conditions read before any account is opened — if the terms are not accessible without registering, the site is rejected.
  2. Live chat interrogation. Before depositing, we ask a substantive question with a checkable answer: the weekly withdrawal cap and the clause containing it. Response time and answer quality are both recorded.
  3. Registration. Real details, GBP currency selected, no bonus claimed on the first pass so the balance is unrestricted.
  4. Identity verification immediately. Photo ID and proof of address uploaded on day one. Time to approval recorded, along with how many resubmissions were required.
  5. Small deposit and withdrawal. £20 in, £10 out, before anything else. This exercises the entire cashier and verification chain at minimal cost, and it is the single most informative test we run.
  6. Games audit. RTP checked in the info panel of the 50 most-played slots and compared against each studio's published maximum figure. Live studios counted at 9pm UK time on a Saturday.
  7. Bonus assessment. On a second pass, the welcome offer is claimed and its terms tested in practice — game weighting, max bet enforcement, wagering tracking accuracy in the cashier.
  8. Mobile testing. Mid-range Android device over UK 4G. Lobby load time, search function, cashier and live dealer stream quality.
  9. Repeat withdrawals. At least three, across at least two payment methods, at different times and days of the week.
  10. Scoring and write-up. Scores entered against the fixed weights. Every review states at least one genuine criticism, because a site with no drawbacks has not been tested properly.

The withdrawal test script

Payouts carry the heaviest weight, so this is the part we standardise most tightly. For each withdrawal we record:

  • T0 — timestamp of the withdrawal request, with a screenshot
  • T1 — timestamp the request leaves "pending" and enters "processing" or "approved"
  • T2 — timestamp funds are confirmed received, from the bank statement, wallet or exchange
  • Method, amount, currency and any fee applied by the operator
  • Day of week and time of day, because card and bank rails behave differently at weekends
  • Whether a bonus was active (for headline figures, never)
  • Any additional documentation requested at the point of withdrawal

Published figures are the median of at least three withdrawals, not the fastest. Where we quote a fastest recorded time — such as Kingdom's 11 minutes — it is labelled as a single best case rather than as a typical result. First withdrawals are excluded from medians because identity verification dominates them; we report those separately.

What disqualifies a site outright

We rejected 31 of 38 sites this year. Any one of these ends the assessment immediately, regardless of how good the rest of the site is:

  • A licence badge that does not resolve to a regulator's own register. The single most common failure.
  • No published company name or registered address.
  • Terms that contradict the promotional banner.
  • "Maximum withdrawal at our discretion" — a cap that is not a number is not a cap.
  • Terms not readable before registration.
  • A failed or unexplained withdrawal during testing.
  • Responsible gambling tools absent, or more than three clicks from the lobby.
  • Games from studios with no verifiable existence.
  • RTP not disclosed in the game info panel.
  • A pattern of credible unpaid-player reports on mediation databases.
  • Pressure mechanics — countdown timers on deposit pages, unsolicited late-night SMS, deposit prompts after a self-imposed limit.
The one that is non-negotiable

If a site does not pay a withdrawal during testing, and does not explain the refusal by reference to a clause we can read, it is removed and it does not come back. We have not published a site that failed this test, and we would rather have a shorter list than a longer one.

When we re-test and re-rank

  • Every quarter as standard, with a full re-run of the withdrawal script and a fresh RTP audit.
  • Immediately when a licence, owner or headline bonus term changes.
  • Immediately on a credible pattern of unpaid-player reports. A site that stops paying comes off the same week — we do not wait for the next cycle.
  • Monthly for bonus terms specifically, because those change most often. The "last updated" date at the top of each page reflects the most recent verification, not the original publication.

Ranking positions move when test results move. Two operators changed position on the homepage between the June and September 2026 passes.

How we make money

Plainly, because you are entitled to know.

When a reader opens an account through a link on this site, the operator pays us a share of the revenue that account generates. This is standard affiliate marketing and it funds everything here: the testing deposits, the writing, the hosting.

Three commitments that constrain it:

  1. The commission rate is identical across every brand we list — 45% revenue share in every case. There is no financial reason for us to rank one operator above another, because we are paid the same either way.
  2. Position cannot be bought. No operator has ever paid for placement, a badge, a score or a mention, and we will not accept such an arrangement.
  3. We publish criticism of every site we recommend, including the top-ranked one. If you read a review here with no drawbacks in it, tell us, because we have failed.

What we do not do: we do not sell tips or predictions, we do not run a VIP or rakeback scheme, and we do not accept payment to remove criticism. More on our about us page.
