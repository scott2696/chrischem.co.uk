Are online casinos legal in the UK?

Yes — and the more useful question is which law applies to whom.

Under the Gambling Act 2005, as amended by the Gambling (Licensing and Advertising) Act 2014, any operator that provides remote gambling facilities to consumers in Great Britain, or advertises to them, must hold a UK Gambling Commission licence. That duty falls on the operator. There is no corresponding offence for a British adult who chooses to play at a casino licensed somewhere else. Playing at an offshore site has never been illegal for you, and no UK player has been prosecuted for it.

So the practical position is:

  • UKGC-licensed casinos — regulated by the Commission, connected to GamStop, subject to the £5 online slot stake limit (£2 for 18–24s), the credit card ban, affordability checks, and IBAS dispute resolution.
  • Offshore casinos, including every site on this page — licensed in Curaçao or Anjouan, outside GamStop, outside stake limits, outside UKGC affordability checks, and with no IBAS route if a dispute goes wrong.

Both are lawful for you to use. They are not equivalent in the protection they give you. The full breakdown, including the 2026 position on the Gambling Act review and the statutory levy, is on the UK online gambling laws page.

What changed in UK gambling in 2025 and 2026

Three reforms have landed since the 2023 white paper, and the most recent one directly inverts an argument this industry has been making for years. If a comparison page has not been updated for 19 January 2026, its bonus advice is out of date.

19 January 2026 — bonus wagering capped at 10x

The Gambling Commission announced on 26 March 2025 that it was amending Social Responsibility Code Provision 5.1.1 (Rewards and bonuses). From 19 January 2026, a UKGC-licensed operator may apply a wagering requirement of no more than 10 times the bonus, and it must apply to bonus funds only — deposit-plus-bonus wagering is finished at licensed sites. The Commission's own worked example was a £10 bonus at 50x requiring £500 of play.

This reverses the usual offshore bonus argument

Affiliate pages have spent years arguing that offshore bonuses beat UK ones. On wagering terms, that is now false. A UKGC site is legally capped at 10x on bonus funds. The offshore sites on this page run 25x to 35x, and two of them apply it to deposit plus bonus. Smash's 10x is no longer market-leading — it merely equals the UK legal maximum. The honest position: offshore still wins on bonus size, game catalogue and RTP builds, and loses on wagering terms, stake caps and recourse. We have corrected every page on this site accordingly.

19 January 2026 — mixed-product incentives banned

A UKGC operator can no longer offer a bonus that requires you to gamble on two different products — placing a sports bet to unlock casino free spins, for example. Offshore operators are not bound by this, and several of the packages on this page do bundle casino and sportsbook offers. Read which product each offer's turnover must be completed on.

9 April and 21 May 2025 — online slot stake limits

A maximum stake of £5 per spin for players aged 25 and over came into force on 9 April 2025, and £2 per spin for players aged 18 to 24 on 21 May 2025. This binds UKGC licensees only. The offshore sites here have no statutory cap, which is a genuine freedom for a controlled player and a genuine hazard for anyone who is not.

6 April 2025 — the statutory levy

Licensed operators now pay a compulsory levy of up to 1.1% of gross gambling yield, ringfenced for research, prevention and treatment. It was expected to raise about £100 million and brought in roughly £120 million in its first year, split 50% to the NHS-led treatment system, 30% to prevention and 20% to UK Research and Innovation. Offshore operators contribute nothing to it — worth knowing when you weigh where your money goes.

What has not changed

The credit card ban (April 2020), mandatory GamStop integration (March 2020), the 2.5-second minimum spin speed and autoplay ban (2021), and the tax-free treatment of your winnings. None of these applies to an offshore operator except the last, which follows you rather than the licence.

Casino bonuses and wagering, explained properly

A welcome bonus is not free money. It is a loan of playing capital with a turnover condition attached, and the condition — not the headline — determines whether the offer is worth taking. Here is the arithmetic every casino advert leaves out.

The wagering calculation

Wagering requirement × bonus amount = the turnover you must complete before withdrawing. A £100 bonus at 35x means £3,500 of bets. At 10x it means £1,000. Since 19 January 2026 a UK-licensed casino cannot exceed 10x, and must apply it to bonus funds only. Offshore sites are not bound by that: the ones on this page run 25x to 35x, which is the industry norm in the offshore market and now well behind the UK legal maximum. Anything above 45x should be declined outright.

Then check what the multiple applies to. "35x bonus" on a £100 bonus is £3,500. "35x deposit plus bonus" on a £100 deposit matched to £100 is £7,000 — double the work for the same headline. Smash's 10x is on deposit plus bonus, which still leaves it far ahead of a 35x-on-bonus rival, but it is worth knowing what you are comparing.

The five terms that actually cost you money

  1. Game weighting. Slots usually count 100% toward wagering. Live dealer, blackjack and roulette often count 10%, sometimes 0%. A "casino bonus" you plan to use on blackjack may need ten times the stated turnover.
  2. Maximum bet while wagering. Typically £5 per spin. Exceed it once, even accidentally on an autoplay setting, and most operators void the bonus and the winnings from it. This is the single most common reason a payout is refused.
  3. Maximum cashout. Common on free spins and no-deposit offers: you may win £400 from 50 spins and be permitted to withdraw £50. Always check this before you value a spins offer.
  4. Expiry. 7 to 30 days is normal. A £10,000 bonus with a 7-day window is not a £10,000 bonus.
  5. Excluded games. Bonus-buy features, jackpot slots and certain high-RTP titles are usually barred while wagering. Playing one can void the bonus outright.

Blackjack

The best odds in the building. Played with correct basic strategy the house edge is around 0.5%, better than any slot ever made. Roulette

Always European (single zero, 97.3% RTP) over American (double zero, 94.74%). Baccarat

The lowest-effort good bet in a casino. Back the Banker at 98.94% RTP after the 5% commission. Do not bet the Tie, which carries a 14.4% house edge and exists to catch people who think the payout looks generous.

Video poker

Full-pay Jacks or Better returns 99.54% with perfect play — among the best odds anywhere. The catch is that most sites carry reduced pay tables, so check the payout for a full house and a flush before you sit down. "9/6" is full pay; "8/5" is not.
