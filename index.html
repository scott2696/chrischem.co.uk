Are online casinos legal in the UK?

Yes — and the more useful question is which law applies to whom.

Under the Gambling Act 2005, as amended by the Gambling (Licensing and Advertising) Act 2014, any operator that provides remote gambling facilities to consumers in Great Britain, or advertises to them, must hold a UK Gambling Commission licence. That duty falls on the operator. There is no corresponding offence for a British adult who chooses to play at a casino licensed somewhere else. Playing at an offshore site has never been illegal for you, and no UK player has been prosecuted for it.

So the practical position is:

  • UKGC-licensed casinos — regulated by the Commission, connected to GamStop, subject to the £5 online slot stake limit (£2 for 18–24s), the credit card ban, affordability checks, and IBAS dispute resolution.

Casino bonuses and wagering, explained properly

A welcome bonus is not free money. It is a loan of playing capital with a turnover condition attached, and the condition — not the headline — determines whether the offer is worth taking. Here is the arithmetic every casino advert leaves out.

The wagering calculation

Wagering requirement × bonus amount = the turnover you must complete before withdrawing. A £100 bonus at 35x means £3,500 of bets. At 10x it means £1,000. The industry norm sits at 35x–40x; anything at or below 25x is genuinely good, and anything above 45x should be declined.

Then check what the multiple applies to. "35x bonus" on a £100 bonus is £3,500. "35x deposit plus bonus" on a £100 deposit matched to £100 is £7,000 — double the work for the same headline. Smash's 10x is on deposit plus bonus, which still leaves it far ahead of a 35x-on-bonus rival, but it is worth knowing what you are comparing.

The five terms that actually cost you money

  1. Game weighting. Slots usually count 100% toward wagering. Live dealer, blackjack and roulette often count 10%, sometimes 0%. A "casino bonus" you plan to use on blackjack may need ten times the stated turnover.
  2. Maximum bet while wagering. Typically £5 per spin. Exceed it once, even accidentally on an autoplay setting, and most operators void the bonus and the winnings from it. This is the single most common reason a payout is refused.
  3. Maximum cashout. Common on free spins and no-deposit offers: you may win £400 from 50 spins and be permitted to withdraw £50. Always check this before you value a spins offer.
  4. Expiry. 7 to 30 days is normal. A £10,000 bonus with a 7-day window is not a £10,000 bonus.
  5. Excluded games. Bonus-buy features, jackpot slots and certain high-RTP titles are usually barred while wagering. Playing one can void the bonus outright.

Do you pay tax on casino winnings in the UK?

No. Gambling winnings are not taxable income for UK players. Betting duty on punters was abolished in 2001 and replaced with a duty on operators — currently Remote Gaming Duty at 21% of gross gaming yield and General Betting Duty at 15%. You do not declare a casino win to HMRC, you do not pay income tax on it, and it does not matter whether you won £50 or £500,000.

This applies to offshore winnings too. The tax treatment follows you, not the operator's licence.

Three edge cases are worth knowing:

  • Interest on your winnings. The winnings are tax-free; the interest they earn in a savings account is taxable in the normal way.
  • Cryptocurrency. If you win in Bitcoin and its sterling value rises before you convert, that gain can fall within Capital Gains Tax. The gambling win is not taxed; the crypto appreciation may be.
  • Inheritance and gifts. Passing a large win on has the usual IHT and gifting consequences.

How to spot an unsafe casino

I rejected 31 of the 38 sites I tested. These are the signals that got them rejected, in the order I check them.

  1. No verifiable licence. A badge in the footer is not a licence. Click it. It should resolve to a regulator's own register with a company name and a live status. If it links nowhere, or to a static image, walk away.
  2. No registered company name or address. Legitimate operators publish the corporate entity and its registered address in the footer or terms. Anonymity is the tell.
  3. Terms that contradict the advert. Compare the promotional banner with the promotion's own terms page. If the banner says "no wagering" and the terms say 35x, you have learned everything you need to know.
  4. Withdrawal caps buried in the terms. A £2,000-per-month withdrawal cap turns a £20,000 jackpot into a ten-month instalment plan. This is legal and it is disclosed — in clause 14.3.
  5. KYC demanded only at withdrawal. A site that verifies nothing at signup and everything at cashout is optimising for the players who give up.
  6. Live chat that does not answer. Ask a hard question before you deposit: "What is the maximum I can withdraw per week and where is that in your terms?" The quality of the answer predicts the quality of the payout.
  7. Pressure mechanics. Countdown timers on deposits, "your bonus expires in 4:59", and reload offers pushed by SMS at midnight are behavioural design, not customer service.

What to do when a casino will not pay

This is the section almost no competitor writes, because it is the part where offshore play is genuinely weaker. Here is the real escalation path.

  1. Live chat, then email. Get it in writing. Ask which specific clause is being relied on. Save the transcript — most chat windows offer an email copy.
  2. Formal complaint to the operator. Every licensed operator, including offshore ones, must have a complaints procedure with a stated response window. Invoke it by name and reference the clause.
  3. The regulator. For Curaçao Gaming Control Board licensees there is a complaints form on the CGCB site; for Anjouan, contact the Anjouan Gaming Authority directly. Be realistic: response times run to weeks, and outcomes are not guaranteed.
  4. Payment provider chargeback. If you funded by debit card, a chargeback through your bank is possible but contentious for gambling transactions and can get your casino account closed. Crypto has no chargeback at all.
  5. Public complaint databases. AskGamblers, Casino Guru and ThePOGG operate mediation services with real leverage over reputation-sensitive operators. In practice these resolve more offshore disputes than regulators do.

Are online casinos legal in the UK?

Yes. The Gambling Act 2005 requires any operator serving Great Britain to hold a UK Gambling Commission licence, but that obligation falls on the operator, not the player. It has never been an offence for a British adult to play at a casino licensed elsewhere, and no UK player has been prosecuted for doing so. Do I pay tax on online casino winnings in the UK?

No. Gambling winnings are not taxable income for UK players. Betting duty on punters was abolished in 2001 and replaced by duties paid by operators.
