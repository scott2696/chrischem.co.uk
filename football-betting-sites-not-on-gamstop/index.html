Every football market, and what it is worth

Match result, double chance and draw no bet

The core market at 101.8% to 104.1% overround across these books. Double chance and draw no bet buy safety, and the price reflects it.

Asian handicap

The lowest-margin football market anywhere at roughly 101.5%. Quarter-goal lines split your stake across two handicaps, so a 0.25 line refunds half on a draw. Available at all except Seven.

Over/under goals and team totals

2.5 is standard; team-specific totals are softer and available at TenoBet, Smash and Kingdom.

Both teams to score

Around 102.2% to 105%. The combined "BTTS and win" variant carries considerably more margin than the legs separately.

Cards and bookings

Total cards, team cards, player to be booked, first card. Referee-dependent, and referee card averages are public. Combine that with fixture context — derbies, relegation six-pointers — and this is the most documentable edge an ordinary punter has.

Corners

Total, handicap and race-to markets. Correlated with crossing volume, wing play and expected possession. Thinly priced below the Premier League.

Player shots and shots on target

The deepest prop category. Premier League lines are tightly modelled; EFL lines are modelled rather than watched, which is where the value survives.

Player assists, tackles and passes

Newer prop markets, available at TenoBet and Smash. Thin liquidity and correspondingly soft pricing.

Goalscorer markets

Anytime, first and last. First goalscorer runs 124% to 133% and exists because the prices look generous. Anytime is materially better value.

Correct score and score-cast

118% to 128%. Avoid.

Half-time markets

Half-time result, half-time/full-time, and first-half goals. Softer than full-match equivalents at several of these books because they are modelled less carefully.

Specials and season-long markets

Top scorer, relegation, top four, manager exit. Wide margins, long settlement, and your stake tied up for months — which also blocks withdrawal of that money.

In-play football betting

How prices update

Every few seconds, driven by a data feed rather than the broadcast. A short acceptance delay while the price is confirmed is standard at every book in the world and is not sharp practice.

The delayed-broadcast problem

The book's feed is faster than your television, and considerably faster than an internet stream. If you are betting in-play off a delayed picture you are structurally behind the price. This is the single biggest practical limitation of offshore books, which carry almost no live streaming.

Markets that suit in-play

Next goal, over/under from the current score, next card, next corner, and Asian handicap re-lined at half-time. All settle quickly and price sharply.

Cash out during in-play

Available at the top five books here. The margin is wider in-play than pre-match, so use it for genuine circumstance changes rather than nerves.

Where the value actually is in football betting

Markets worth your attention

  • Player props in the EFL. The Premier League is the most efficiently priced market in world sport — every trading desk on earth models it. League One shots-on-target lines are modelled, not watched, and that is where mispricing survives. TenoBet and Smash carry the deepest lower-league prop lists.
  • Cards. Heavily referee-dependent. Referee card averages are public, team disciplinary records are public, and derby fixtures skew heavily. This is the most documentable edge available to an ordinary punter.
  • Corners. Strongly correlated with team style — wide play, crossing volume, expected possession. Thinly priced at smaller books.
  • Asian handicaps on mismatches. Removing the draw sharpens the price and cuts the margin from ~102.5% to ~101.5%.
  • Women's Super League. Thin modelling at most books. TenoBet and Smash are the only two here that price it properly.

Markets to leave alone

  • Correct score — 118–128% overround.
  • First goalscorer — 124–133%.
  • Long accumulators — margin compounds per leg. A five-fold at 2.5% per leg carries roughly 13% against you.
  • Bet builders — correlated legs priced in the book's favour.
  • Premier League match result — you will not consistently beat it.

In-play and bet builders

In-play

The honest caveat: live streaming is limited at all seven, which is the clearest disadvantage against UK bookmakers. If you are betting in-play off a delayed broadcast stream you are structurally behind the price, because the book's data feed is faster than your television. Most punters here watch elsewhere and bet on the phone; that works, but only if your feed is genuinely live.

Bet builders

Available at six of seven. Combine team to win, player to score, over 9.5 corners and so on into one price. Convenient and popular. Understand that the legs are correlated and the book prices that correlation in its own favour, so a bet builder is almost always worse value than the equivalent legs placed separately. Treat it as entertainment rather than as strategy.

Are the odds better at non GamStop football books?

Which football markets offer the best value?

Player props in the EFL, where books model rather than watch; cards, which are strongly referee-dependent and documentable from public data; corners, which correlate with team style; and Asian handicaps on mismatched fixtures, which carry the lowest margin of any football market at around 101.5%. Avoid correct score at 118–128% overround and first goalscorer at 124–133%.

Do these bookmakers cover lower league football?

Can I bet in-play on football at these sites?

Yes, with full in-play coverage at TenoBet, Smash, Kingdom, Rivo and Gambiva. The significant limitation is live streaming, which is minimal at all seven — a clear disadvantage against UK bookmakers. If you bet in-play off a delayed broadcast you are structurally behind the price, because the book's data feed arrives faster than your television picture.

Are bet builders good value?

Rarely. The legs of a bet builder are correlated — a team winning makes their striker scoring more likely — and the bookmaker prices that correlation in its own favour. The result is almost always worse value than the equivalent selections placed separately where that is possible. They are convenient and enjoyable rather than profitable.

What is an Asian handicap and why does it have the best odds?

An Asian handicap removes the draw by giving one team a goal start, sometimes in quarter-goal increments that split your stake across two lines. Because there are only two outcomes rather than three, the bookmaker's risk is simpler and the margin is lower — roughly 101.5% against 102.5% or more on the three-way market. On mismatched fixtures it is consistently the best-value way to back a favourite.

Are card and corner markets worth betting?

They are the most documentable edge available to an ordinary punter. Card totals are heavily referee-dependent and referee card averages are public data, so combining a referee's record with fixture context — derbies, relegation matches — is genuine analysis rather than guesswork. Corners correlate with crossing volume and wing play. Both are thinly priced below the Premier League at smaller books.

Why is in-play betting harder at offshore books?

Live streaming is minimal at all seven offshore books we test, so most punters watch on television or an internet stream and bet on a phone. Both are delayed relative to the bookmaker's data feed — often by several seconds. Betting in-play off a delayed picture puts you structurally behind the price. It is the clearest practical disadvantage of offshore books against UK ones for in-play punters.

How quickly are football bets settled?

Match result, over/under and both-teams-to-score markets settled within ten minutes of the final whistle at TenoBet, Smash and Kingdom, and within twenty minutes elsewhere. Player props take up to an hour because they depend on official data feeds being confirmed, which is standard at every bookmaker. Cards and corners settle in 15 to 30 minutes.
