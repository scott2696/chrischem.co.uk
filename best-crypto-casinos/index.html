Getting started from the UK, step by step

If you have never used cryptocurrency, this takes about twenty minutes the first time and two minutes every time after.

  1. Open an exchange account. Kraken, Coinbase and Bitstamp all operate legally in the UK and are registered with the FCA for anti-money-laundering purposes. Verification is the same photo ID and proof of address the casino will ask for.
  2. Deposit pounds. Faster Payments bank transfer is free and near-instant on all three. Note that some UK banks decline card payments to exchanges, so use bank transfer.
  3. Buy USDT. Not Bitcoin, for the reasons above. Buy a little more than you plan to deposit to cover fees.
  4. Withdraw to the casino's deposit address. Copy the address from the casino cashier, select the matching network — TRC-20 for TRON — and send. Send a small test amount first the very first time. Everyone who has ever lost crypto to a wrong-network transfer skipped this step.
  5. Play. Withdraw to the same exchange address. Then sell back to GBP and transfer to your bank.
Three things that will cost you money

Wrong network. USDT exists on TRON, Ethereum, Solana and others. The network must match at both ends or the funds are gone permanently. Wrong coin. Sending Litecoin to a Bitcoin address is unrecoverable. Bank scrutiny. Some UK banks flag exchange transfers, particularly returning ones. Keep records of every transaction — it is your money and it is entirely lawful, but you may be asked to explain it.

What it costs you — the honest list

  • It is not anonymous. This is the biggest myth in the category. You still complete full KYC at the casino, and your exchange has your identity too. Crypto changes the payment rail, not your obligations.
  • Price volatility. A £500 Bitcoin withdrawal can be worth £470 by the time it lands. Stablecoins solve this entirely and you should use them.
  • No chargeback, ever. A debit card dispute is a real, if contentious, route to recovering funds. Crypto has no equivalent. Once sent, it is gone.
  • Irreversible mistakes. One wrong character in an address, or the wrong network, and the money is unrecoverable by anyone.
  • It bypasses your own safety controls. If you turned on your bank's gambling block, crypto defeats it. That matters, and it is the reason I would steer anyone who has ever worried about their gambling away from crypto casinos entirely. See responsible gambling.
  • Capital Gains Tax exposure. Your winnings are tax-free; an increase in the sterling value of the crypto before you convert may not be. Covered on the tax page.

Provably fair games explained

Crypto casinos often carry in-house games — dice, crash, plinko, mines — labelled "provably fair". The mechanism is genuine and worth understanding.

Before a round, the casino generates a random server seed and shows you its cryptographic hash. You supply or are given a client seed. The outcome is computed from both. Afterwards, the casino reveals the original server seed, and you can hash it yourself to confirm it matches what you were shown before the round. Because the hash was published in advance, the casino could not have changed the seed after seeing your bet.

What this proves: the operator did not manipulate that specific round. What it does not prove: that the house edge is fair. A provably fair dice game can have a 15% edge and still be provably fair. Check the stated house edge as well — the good ones run at 1%.

Note that classic slots from Pragmatic Play, Evolution and the rest are not provably fair. They use certified RNGs audited by testing houses instead. Both are legitimate; they are different mechanisms.

Crypto gambling and UK tax

Two separate questions, and the answer differs.

  • Your gambling winnings: not taxable. Same as any other gambling win in the UK. HMRC does not treat recreational gambling as taxable income, and it makes no difference that the win was denominated in Bitcoin.
  • Your crypto's change in value: potentially taxable. Cryptoassets are property for CGT purposes. If you buy £1,000 of Bitcoin, win with it, withdraw, and the Bitcoin is worth £1,400 in sterling when you sell it, that £400 gain can be a chargeable gain against your annual exempt amount.

Using a stablecoin removes this problem almost entirely, since USDT tracks the dollar and any movement is a currency effect rather than a meaningful gain. Keep a record of your transactions either way. Full detail on the gambling winnings tax page. This is general information, not tax advice — check with an accountant if the amounts are significant.
