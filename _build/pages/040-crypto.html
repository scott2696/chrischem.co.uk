Provably fair games explained

Crypto casinos often carry in-house games — dice, crash, plinko, mines — labelled "provably fair". The mechanism is genuine and worth understanding.

Before a round, the casino generates a random server seed and shows you its cryptographic hash. You supply or are given a client seed. The outcome is computed from both. Afterwards, the casino reveals the original server seed, and you can hash it yourself to confirm it matches what you were shown before the round. Because the hash was published in advance, the casino could not have changed the seed after seeing your bet.

What this proves: the operator did not manipulate that specific round. What it does not prove: that the house edge is fair. A provably fair dice game can have a 15% edge and still be provably fair. Check the stated house edge as well — the good ones run at 1%.

Note that classic slots from Pragmatic Play, Evolution and the rest are not provably fair. They use certified RNGs audited by testing houses instead. Both are legitimate; they are different mechanisms.

Crypto gambling and UK tax

Two separate questions, and the answer differs.

  • Your gambling winnings: not taxable. Same as any other gambling win in the UK. HMRC does not treat recreational gambling as taxable income, and it makes no difference that the win was denominated in Bitcoin.
  • Your crypto's change in value: potentially taxable. Cryptoassets are property for CGT purposes. If you buy £1,000 of Bitcoin, win with it, withdraw, and the Bitcoin is worth £1,400 in sterling when you sell it, that £400 gain can be a chargeable gain against your annual exempt amount.

Using a stablecoin removes this problem almost entirely, since USDT tracks the dollar and any movement is a currency effect rather than a meaningful gain. Keep a record of your transactions either way. Full detail on the gambling winnings tax page. This is general information, not tax advice — check with an accountant if the amounts are significant.
