Why your withdrawal is pending

This is the most-searched question in the category and it deserves a direct answer. In order of how often it is the actual cause:

  1. Identity verification is incomplete. Roughly two-thirds of delayed withdrawals. The casino will not release funds until KYC passes, and it usually only starts KYC when you first ask for money.
  2. A bonus is still active with wagering outstanding. Your withdrawable balance and your total balance are different numbers. Check the cashier for "withdrawable" specifically.
  3. The operator's standard pending window. 0 to 72 hours depending on the site. Disclosed in the terms, usually under "Withdrawal Policy".
  4. You exceeded a daily or weekly cap. The excess is queued to the next period rather than refused, so it looks like a delay.
  5. Withdrawal method mismatch. Most operators require you to withdraw to the method you deposited with, up to the deposited amount, for anti-money-laundering reasons. Depositing by paysafecard and requesting a card payout will stall.
  6. A manual review on a large amount. Above roughly £2,000 many offshore sites add a compliance check. This is normal and usually adds 24 to 48 hours.
  7. Weekend or bank holiday. Applies to cards and bank transfers only.

If none of those applies and you are past 72 hours, escalate in writing through live chat and ask which specific clause is being relied on. The complaints escalation path is set out on our homepage.

Anti-money-laundering rules at almost every operator require funds to return the way they came, at least up to the amount deposited. Planning your deposit method around your intended withdrawal method removes an entire class of delay.

For cards and bank transfers only. Crypto and open banking do not care what day it is.

Never cancel a pending withdrawal

The "cancel withdrawal" button exists to return funds to your playable balance. Casinos with long pending windows know exactly what they are doing when they place it prominently, and the industry term for the money recovered this way is "reverse withdrawal revenue". Is "instant withdrawal" real?

Mostly no, and the phrase is doing a lot of work in casino marketing. Here is what it actually means in practice:

  • "Instant" usually means the operator's approval step is automated rather than manual. That removes the pending window but not the payment rail's own settlement time.
  • The genuinely near-instant case is crypto on a verified account with no bonus active. My 11-minute Kingdom payout was exactly this. Most of those 11 minutes were blockchain confirmations, not the casino.
  • "Instant withdrawal, no verification" does not exist at any legitimate operator. Every licensed site, offshore included, must verify identity before releasing funds. A site promising otherwise is either lying or is not running AML controls, and neither is a site you want holding your money.

Payment methods to avoid if speed matters

  • paysafecard — deposit only. You will need a second verified method to get paid, and setting that up after you win adds days.
  • Bank transfer — the slowest rail, with a median of three working days and a worst case of nine.
  • Credit card — cannot be used for withdrawals at all, and should not be used for deposits either. Your issuer treats it as a cash advance with interest from day one.
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay — deposit-only wrappers around your debit card. Fine going in, useless coming out.

Verification: what they ask for and why

Every operator, offshore included, must confirm you are who you say you are before releasing money. It is anti-money-laundering law, not obstruction. What you will need:

  • Photo ID — passport or UK photocard driving licence. Full page, all four corners visible, no glare.
  • Proof of address — bank statement, council tax bill or utility bill dated within three months, showing your name and address. Mobile phone bills are usually rejected.
  • Proof of payment method — a card photo with the middle digits obscured, or an e-wallet account screenshot.
  • Source of funds — only for large amounts or unusual patterns. Payslips or bank statements. Being asked this is normal above roughly £5,000 and is not an accusation.

Photograph documents flat on a dark surface in daylight. Roughly a third of rejected uploads fail on glare or a cropped corner alone, and each rejection costs you another 24 hours.
