# Competitor analysis — "best online casinos", UK focus
**Compiled 2 September 2026.** Markets sampled: UK, US, AU, CA, NZ. The UK
column is the target; the other four are read for tactics worth importing.

| Market | Pages that own the head term | Page archetype |

| **US** | casino.org, covers.com, actionnetwork | State-by-state legality gating everything |

| **NZ** | casino.org/new-zealand, gambling.com/nz | Offshore-tolerant, DIA licensing timeline |

Two distinct playbooks are visible:

- **Regulated-market playbook (UK, US, CA-Ontario).** Thin editorial, heavy
  compliance furniture, brand-grid first, trust signals borrowed from the
  regulator. Content is shallow because legal review is expensive.
- **Offshore-tolerant playbook (AU, NZ, and the UK non-GamStop segment).**
  Long pages, deep H3 nesting, big FAQ blocks, aggressive bonus framing, and
  almost no honest discussion of what the player gives up.

**The UK gap sits between them:** nobody is writing offshore-market content
with regulated-market rigour.

## 2. What the top pages do well

1. **Brand grid above the fold.** Rank, logo, offer, rating, CTA. Universal, and
   it works — imitate the pattern, not the emptiness.
2. **Comparison table with 6–8 columns.** Present on every ranking page.
3. **"Best for X" segmentation.** Best for bonuses / payouts / slots / live.
   Captures modifier long-tails cheaply.
4. **Heavy FAQ blocks.** 11–14 questions on the strongest pages, all with
   FAQPage schema. This is where they win People Also Ask.
5. **Named reviewer with a photo** (casino.org, casinos.com). Real E-E-A-T
   signal, and most UK competitors have it.
6. **Published methodology page** with weighted criteria.
7. **"Last updated" plus a next-review date.** Freshness signalling.
8. **Deep H3 nesting.** 8–34 H3s per money page. This is the single biggest
   structural difference between page 1 and page 3.
9. **Per-method payout ranges** with real numbers (casinos.com does this best).
10. **RG furniture everywhere** — helpline, 18+, BeGambleAware.
