Football betting

Football is roughly 60% of all UK sports betting turnover, and it is where these books are judged. What I found across a Premier League matchweek:

Markets that matter

  • Match result, both teams to score, over/under goals — universal, and priced within a point or two of the UK high street across all seven.
  • Asian handicap — better value than the three-way market on mismatched fixtures, because the draw is removed. Available on all except Seven.
  • Player props — shots, shots on target, cards, assists, tackles. TenoBet and Smash carry the deepest lists; this is where the biggest pricing errors appear, and where a punter who watches a lot of football has an actual edge.
  • Corners and cards — heavily bet in the UK and thinly priced at smaller books, which cuts both ways.
  • Bet builder / same game multi — combine legs from one fixture. Note that correlated legs are priced with a margin that is usually worse than the individual legs combined. Convenient, rarely value.

Horse racing

Racing is the category where offshore books are weakest against the UK high street, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

  • What you get: UK and Irish daily cards at TenoBet, Smash, Kingdom and Gambiva, with win, each-way and forecast markets, plus the major festivals — Cheltenham, Aintree, Royal Ascot, Glorious Goodwood, the Guineas and the Derby.
  • What you do not get: Best Odds Guaranteed as standard, which the UK high street offers routinely and which is worth roughly 3–5% on ante-post and morning-price bets over a season. Non-runner-no-bet on ante-post festival markets is also less consistently applied.
  • Rule 4 deductions apply as normal on non-runners, at the standard tariff.
  • Each-way terms are generally the industry standard — a quarter the odds, places one to four depending on field size — but enhanced place terms on big handicaps are rarer than at UK books.

The offshore case is stronger on football, in-play, esports and — crucially — on not restricting your account when you win. Pick the book for the sport, not the other way round.

Cash out, bet builder and BOG

Cash out

Settle a bet early for a value the book calculates. Available on all seven for pre-match singles and most accumulators, and in-play at the top four. Understand what it costs: the cash out figure always carries an additional margin over the true current value of your position. It is a convenience product, and taking it consistently costs you money over time. Use it when circumstances genuinely change — a red card, an injury — not out of nerves.

Bet builder

Combine multiple markets from one fixture into a single price. Available at six of seven. The legs are correlated, and the book prices that correlation in its own favour, so a bet builder is almost always worse value than the equivalent legs placed separately where that is possible. Fun, popular, rarely optimal.

Best Odds Guaranteed

The UK high street standard on horse racing: if the starting price beats the price you took, you are paid at SP. None of the books on this page offers BOG as standard, and that is the single clearest disadvantage against a UKGC bookmaker for a racing punter. Worth 3–5% over a season on morning-price bets.

Account restrictions: the UK punter's real problem

This is the section that explains why offshore books have a UK audience at all, and no competitor page states it plainly.

UK bookmakers are commercially free to restrict or close any account they choose. In practice, a punter who wins consistently — particularly on horse racing at early prices, or on arbitrage — will find maximum stakes cut to a few pounds, sometimes within weeks. This is legal, ubiquitous and the single most common complaint about UK bookmakers. The Gambling Commission does not regulate stake limits set for commercial reasons.

Offshore books, competing for volume and less concerned with liability on UK racing, restrict far less aggressively. No. Betting duty on punters was abolished in 2001 and replaced with duties paid by operators. Winnings from sports betting are not taxable income regardless of amount, and this applies to offshore winnings too. Full detail is on our gambling winnings tax page.
