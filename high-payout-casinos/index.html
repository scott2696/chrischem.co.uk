What "high payout" actually means

Two completely different things get called "payout" in casino marketing, and conflating them costs players money.

Payout percentage is RTP — the share of total stakes a game returns over its lifetime. This is a mathematical property of the game, published by the studio, verifiable in the info panel. A 96.5% slot returns £96.50 per £100 staked over millions of spins. This page is about that.

Payout speed is how quickly the casino sends your money once you ask for it. Entirely separate, entirely a property of the operator, and covered on the fast payout casinos page.

A site can be excellent at one and poor at the other. Kingdom happens to lead both, which is why it tops this page and that one.

The spread looks small — 0.6 percentage points between first and last — and on a single spin it is meaningless. Over £10,000 of turnover, which a regular player reaches in a few months, it is £60. Over a year of weekly play it is real money, and it costs you nothing to be on the right side of it.

The RTP versions problem — the thing nobody explains

This is the most important paragraph on the page, and I have not found a single competing UK casino guide that covers it properly.

Since roughly 2019, most major slot studios have shipped their games in multiple RTP configurations. Pragmatic Play, Play'n GO, Red Tiger, NetEnt and Blueprint all do it. The same slot — same art, same name, same bonus round, same maths in every visible respect — exists at 96.5%, at 94.5%, at 92% and sometimes at 88%. The operator chooses which build to license, and the choice is usually commercial: a lower RTP version pays the casino more.

How to verify a slot's RTP yourself

  1. Open the game.
  2. Tap the menu or "i" icon, usually bottom-left or in the burger menu.
  3. Scroll to the paytable's last page or the "Game Rules" section.
  4. Look for "The theoretical return to player is X%". That figure is the build you are being served, right now, on this site.
  5. Compare it to the studio's published maximum. A quick search for "[game name] RTP [studio]" gives you the top figure.

If the number is more than one percentage point below the studio maximum, close the game and play it somewhere else. Do this once for each of your three or four regular titles and you never need to think about it again.

Why high RTP alone will not save your bankroll

RTP tells you the long-run average. It tells you nothing about the next hour. Two 96% slots can behave completely differently:

  • Low volatility: frequent small wins, gentle balance decline, sessions last. Good for stretching £20 across an evening and for clearing wagering.
  • High volatility: long dry spells punctuated by large hits. A £20 balance frequently disappears in under ten minutes. The 96% is still true — it is just delivered in occasional 400x lumps you may never personally see.

The right combination depends on your goal. Chasing a big win: high volatility, accept the variance, stake small. Making an evening last: low volatility, high RTP, and set a loss limit before you start.

What is a good RTP for an online slot?

96% or above is good, 95–96% is average, and below 94% is poor for a modern video slot. Anything above 97% is exceptional. The very best available titles reach 98–99%: Mega Joker at 99.07%, Book of 99 at 99.00%, and 1429 Uncharted Seas at 98.60%. Note that progressive jackpot slots run much lower, typically 88–94%, because base-game return is cut to fund the jackpot pool.

Can a casino change a slot's RTP?

Not while you are playing, and not for a specific player. But the operator does choose which RTP build to license from the studio, and most major studios ship the same game at several settings — Starburst exists at both 96.09% and 90.05%. Always check the figure in the game's own info panel, because it reflects the build being served on that site, not the studio's headline.

Which casino game has the highest payout?

Do high RTP slots pay out more often?

Not necessarily — that is volatility, which is a different property. RTP is the long-run percentage returned; volatility is how that return is distributed. A high-RTP, high-volatility slot pays rarely and large. A high-RTP, low-volatility slot pays often and small. For making a bankroll last or clearing a bonus, you want high RTP combined with low volatility.
