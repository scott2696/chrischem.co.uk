What "high payout" actually means

Two completely different things get called "payout" in casino marketing, and conflating them costs players money.

Payout percentage is RTP — the share of total stakes a game returns over its lifetime. This is a mathematical property of the game, published by the studio, verifiable in the info panel. A 96.5% slot returns £96.50 per £100 staked over millions of spins. This page is about that.

Payout speed is how quickly the casino sends your money once you ask for it. Entirely separate, entirely a property of the operator, and covered on the fast payout casinos page.

A site can be excellent at one and poor at the other. Kingdom happens to lead both, which is why it tops this page and that one.

How to verify a slot's RTP yourself

  1. Open the game.
  2. Tap the menu or "i" icon, usually bottom-left or in the burger menu.
  3. Scroll to the paytable's last page or the "Game Rules" section.
  4. Look for "The theoretical return to player is X%". That figure is the build you are being served, right now, on this site.
  5. Compare it to the studio's published maximum. A quick search for "[game name] RTP [studio]" gives you the top figure.

If the number is more than one percentage point below the studio maximum, close the game and play it somewhere else. Do this once for each of your three or four regular titles and you never need to think about it again.

How RTP is calculated and audited

What the number means

RTP is the theoretical percentage of all money staked that a game returns over its lifetime — typically simulated across billions of spins by the studio. It is a property of the game's mathematics, not a forecast for your session.

Who checks it

Independent testing houses — eCOGRA, iTech Labs and GLI — verify that a game's random number generator is genuinely random and that its actual return matches the published figure. A site carrying certified games from named studios is running audited maths.

Theoretical versus actual RTP

Some operators publish an "actual RTP" for the last month alongside the theoretical figure. Over a month, actual RTP swings wildly — a single jackpot can push a game above 200% — so it tells you nothing useful about your chances. The theoretical figure is the one to use.

Why RTP says nothing about a session

Over a thousand spins, a 96% RTP slot's realistic outcomes range from losing everything to multiplying your stake many times. The 96% only asserts itself across a sample far larger than any individual will ever play. Understanding this is the difference between informed play and superstition.

Volatility, hit frequency and max win

Low volatility

Frequent small wins, gentle balance decline, long sessions. Hit frequency often above 30%. The right choice for stretching £20 across an evening and the efficient choice for clearing a bonus.

Medium volatility

The middle ground most popular titles occupy. Hit frequency around 20–25%.

High volatility

Long dry spells and occasional very large hits. Hit frequency can fall below 15%. A £20 balance frequently disappears in under ten minutes. The 96% is still true — it is simply delivered in rare lumps you may personally never see.

Hit frequency versus RTP

Two different measurements that players routinely conflate. Hit frequency is how often any win lands; RTP is how much comes back overall. A game can hit on 40% of spins and still return 94%, because most of those hits are below your stake.

Maximum win multiplier

The cap on what a single spin can return, from around 500x on a low-volatility title to 50,000x on the extreme end. A high max win is always paired with a low hit frequency — the maths does not permit otherwise.

Why high RTP alone will not save your bankroll

RTP tells you the long-run average. It tells you nothing about the next hour. Two 96% slots can behave completely differently:

  • Low volatility: frequent small wins, gentle balance decline, sessions last. Good for stretching £20 across an evening and for clearing wagering.
  • High volatility: long dry spells punctuated by large hits. A £20 balance frequently disappears in under ten minutes. The 96% is still true — it is just delivered in occasional 400x lumps you may never personally see.

The right combination depends on your goal. Chasing a big win: high volatility, accept the variance, stake small. Making an evening last: low volatility, high RTP, and set a loss limit before you start.

What is a good RTP for an online slot?

96% or above is good, 95–96% is average, and below 94% is poor for a modern video slot. Anything above 97% is exceptional. The very best available titles reach 98–99%: Mega Joker at 99.07%, Book of 99 at 99.00%, and 1429 Uncharted Seas at 98.60%. Note that progressive jackpot slots run much lower, typically 88–94%, because base-game return is cut to fund the jackpot pool.

Can a casino change a slot's RTP?

Not while you are playing, and not for a specific player. But the operator does choose which RTP build to license from the studio, and most major studios ship the same game at several settings — Starburst exists at both 96.09% and 90.05%. Always check the figure in the game's own info panel, because it reflects the build being served on that site, not the studio's headline.

Which casino game has the highest payout?

Blackjack played with correct basic strategy, at roughly 99.5% RTP. Full-pay Jacks or Better video poker is comparable at 99.54% with perfect play, and Seven Casino's Infinite Blackjack table runs at 99.60%. Among slots, Mega Joker reaches 99.07% in supermeter mode and Book of 99 hits 99.00%.

Do high RTP slots pay out more often?

Not necessarily — that is volatility, which is a different property. RTP is the long-run percentage returned; volatility is how that return is distributed. A high-RTP, high-volatility slot pays rarely and large. A high-RTP, low-volatility slot pays often and small. For making a bankroll last or clearing a bonus, you want high RTP combined with low volatility.

Are offshore casinos really higher RTP than UK-licensed ones?

In my testing, generally yes — offshore operators served the studio's top RTP build more consistently than UK-licensed sites, which more often licensed reduced builds. This is a genuine measurable advantage. Set it against what you give up: no GamStop, no £5 stake cap, no IBAS dispute resolution. The full comparison is on our best online casinos UK page.

What is the difference between RTP and hit frequency?

RTP is the percentage of total stakes a game returns over its lifetime. Hit frequency is how often any winning combination lands, regardless of size. They are independent: a slot can hit on 40% of spins and still return only 94%, because most of those hits pay less than the stake. Low-volatility games have high hit frequency and modest payouts; high-volatility games have low hit frequency and rare large payouts.

Who audits casino RTP figures?

Independent testing houses — principally eCOGRA, iTech Labs and GLI — verify that a game's random number generator produces genuinely random outcomes and that its actual return matches the published theoretical RTP. Certification is issued to the studio for the game and, separately, to operators for their platforms. A lobby stocked with certified games from named studios is running audited mathematics.

Should I trust a casino's published monthly payout percentage?

Treat it as a minor trust signal rather than a guide to your chances. Actual RTP measured over a single month swings enormously — one progressive jackpot can push a game above 200% for that period — so it tells you little about the underlying mathematics. The theoretical RTP in the game info panel is the figure that governs your expected return, and it is the one to check.

How do I find the RTP of a slot before I play it?

Open the game, tap the menu or "i" icon, and scroll to the last page of the paytable or the Game Rules section. It will state "The theoretical return to player is X%". That figure is the build being served on that specific site. Compare it against the studio's published maximum, which a quick search will give you.
