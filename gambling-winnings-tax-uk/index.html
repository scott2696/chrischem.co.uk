The short answer: Gambling winnings are not taxable income in the United Kingdom. Betting duty on punters was abolished in 2001 and replaced with duties paid by operators — currently General Betting Duty at 15% and Remote Gaming Duty at 21% of operator gross profit. You do not declare a gambling win to HMRC regardless of size, there is no threshold, and it makes no difference whether the operator is UK-licensed or offshore. Even professional gamblers pay no income tax on gambling profits, because gambling is not treated as a trade in UK law. The four edge cases: interest earned on the money afterwards, cryptoasset appreciation, inheritance and gifting, and income from gambling-adjacent activities such as tipping services or streaming.

Why UK gambling winnings are tax-free

Two reasons, one historic and one doctrinal.

The historic reason. Until 2001 punters paid a 6.75% betting duty, usually deducted from the stake or the returns in a betting shop. In the March 2001 Budget the Chancellor abolished it, replacing it with a duty on bookmakers' gross profits. The stated aim was to stop British betting turnover migrating offshore, and it worked. The consequence for you is that the tax point moved from the punter to the operator, and it has stayed there.

The doctrinal reason. UK tax law does not treat gambling as a trade. The principle traces back to Graham v Green (1925), where the court held that a man who lived by betting on horses was not carrying on a trade, because his winnings arose from the exercise of judgment on chance events rather than from a commercial undertaking. HMRC's own guidance follows this: gambling winnings are not chargeable to income tax, and correspondingly gambling losses are not deductible.

That symmetry is the key to understanding the whole position. Because you cannot offset losses, HMRC does not tax gains. Treating gambling as a trade would let the vast majority of gamblers — who lose — claim relief, at enormous cost to the Exchequer.

What counts as gambling winnings

All of the following are tax-free for a UK player:

  • Online slots, table games and live dealer wins
  • Progressive jackpots, of any size
  • Sports betting and horse racing returns
  • Poker winnings, including tournament prizes
  • Bingo prizes
  • National Lottery, EuroMillions and scratchcard prizes
  • Casino comps, cashback and converted loyalty points
  • Matched betting profits from free bets and bonuses
  • Spread betting profits (financial spread betting is treated as gambling, unlike CFDs)

There is no threshold, no declaration, and nothing to put on a self-assessment return. A £10 win and a £10 million win receive identical treatment.

Offshore and foreign winnings

The tax treatment follows you, not the operator's licence. A win at a Curaçao-licensed casino is as tax-free in your hands as a win at a UKGC-licensed one, because the exemption arises from the nature of gambling in UK law rather than from where the operator sits.

Two practical notes:

  • Some countries withhold tax at source. The United States withholds 30% on certain gambling winnings for non-residents. The UK–US double taxation treaty allows UK residents to reclaim this, which requires an ITIN and a US tax filing. This is a US matter, not a UK one, and it does not arise with the offshore operators covered on this site.
  • Your bank may ask questions about a large incoming payment. That is anti-money-laundering procedure, not taxation. Keep evidence of the win — a screenshot of the transaction history and the withdrawal confirmation is usually enough.

Cryptocurrency winnings

This is where the answer becomes genuinely two-part, and it is the most misunderstood area on the page.

The gambling win itself: not taxable

Winning 0.05 BTC on a slot is a gambling win, and gambling wins are not taxable income in the UK. That does not change because the denomination is a cryptoasset.

The cryptoasset's change in value: potentially taxable

HMRC treats cryptoassets as property for Capital Gains Tax purposes. Disposing of a cryptoasset — selling it for pounds, exchanging it for another coin, or using it to buy something — is a disposal, and any gain in its sterling value between acquisition and disposal is a chargeable gain against your annual exempt amount.

Worked through:

  1. You buy £1,000 of Bitcoin. Acquisition cost: £1,000.
  2. You deposit it, play, and withdraw the same quantity of Bitcoin.
  3. Bitcoin has risen. You sell for £1,400.
  4. The £400 gain is a potential chargeable gain. The gambling activity is irrelevant to this — the same gain would arise had you simply held it.
How to make this a non-issue

Use a dollar-pegged stablecoin such as USDT. Its sterling value moves only with the pound–dollar rate, so meaningful gains do not accumulate, and it settles faster and cheaper than Bitcoin anyway. Keep a simple record of acquisitions and disposals either way — date, quantity, sterling value. Full guidance on best crypto casinos. This is general information rather than tax advice; if the amounts are significant, speak to an accountant.

Professional gamblers

Even a full-time professional pays no income tax on gambling winnings in the UK. The principle from Graham v Green holds regardless of scale, skill or regularity: gambling is not a trade, so its profits are not trading income. HMRC's Business Income Manual is explicit that the fact of being a skilful gambler does not by itself create a trade.

What is taxable is income from activities around gambling:

  • Selling tips or predictions — trading income
  • Streaming or content creation, including sponsorship and affiliate commission — trading income
  • Appearance fees, sponsorship or coaching — taxable
  • Being employed by a betting syndicate — employment income
  • Trading on betting exchanges as a business with organised systems — the boundary here is genuinely contested and worth professional advice

The distinction is between winning at gambling, which is not taxed, and earning money from the activity of gambling, which is.

Tax treatment by gambling activity

Online casino and slots

Not taxable. No declaration, no threshold, no difference between £50 and £500,000.

Sports betting and horse racing

Not taxable, including accumulator wins and ante-post payouts.

Poker — cash games and tournaments

Not taxable, including live tournament prizes won abroad by a UK resident. Note that some countries withhold at source; see foreign winnings.

Bingo and the National Lottery

Not taxable. EuroMillions and scratchcard prizes likewise.

Spread betting

Financial spread betting is treated as gambling and is not taxable. Contracts for difference are not gambling and are within Capital Gains Tax — a distinction that catches people.

Matched betting

Not taxable. The profits arise from bookmaker free bets and bonuses and retain their character as gambling winnings.

Casino comps, cashback and loyalty conversions

Not taxable, being incidental to gambling rather than earned income.

Esports and fantasy sports

Betting on esports is gambling and untaxed. Prize money won by competing in an esports tournament is not gambling — it is income from an activity, and it is taxable.

Crypto casino winnings

The win is not taxable; the cryptoasset's change in sterling value between acquisition and disposal may be. See the crypto section.

Offshore online casinos

Identical treatment to a UK-licensed site. The exemption arises from how UK law characterises gambling, not from where the operator sits.

The US withholds 30% at source on certain gambling winnings paid to non-residents, including some poker tournament prizes. Most European countries do not withhold from UK residents. Where one does, check the relevant double taxation treaty before assuming the deduction is final.

Bringing money back to the UK

No tax event. Your bank may ask about a large credit under anti-money-laundering procedures — keep the withdrawal confirmation and transaction history and it resolves quickly.

The professional gambler question, in detail

The leading authority

Graham v Green (1925) established that a man who lived by betting on horses was not carrying on a trade. His winnings arose from the exercise of judgment on chance events rather than from a commercial undertaking, so there was no trading income to tax.

HMRC's stated position

The Business Income Manual is explicit that the fact of being a skilful gambler does not by itself create a trade, and that an organised approach, record keeping and consistent profit do not change the analysis.

Why the rule survives

Because it cuts both ways. If gambling were a trade, the overwhelming majority of gamblers — who lose — could claim loss relief against other income. The cost to the Exchequer would far exceed the tax collected from the few who win.

Where the line actually is

Not at skill or scale, but at whether you are being paid for something other than the gamble itself. Selling tips, streaming, sponsorship, coaching, affiliate commission and employment by a syndicate are all taxable trading or employment income. Betting exchange trading operated with organised systems and infrastructure is genuinely contested territory and warrants professional advice.

Practical matters after a large win

Telling your bank

Not required, but a large unexplained credit will often trigger a routine enquiry. Volunteering the withdrawal confirmation up front avoids a frozen payment.

Interest and investment

The win is tax-free; interest it earns is savings income, taxable in the normal way subject to your Personal Savings Allowance.

Inheritance tax

The money forms part of your estate and is subject to IHT above the nil-rate band. "Tax-free" means untaxed when received, not permanently outside the tax system.

Gifting

Lifetime gifts engage the seven-year rule for potentially exempt transfers. Annual and small-gift exemptions apply as normal.

Means-tested benefits

Gambling winnings are capital. Capital above the thresholds affects entitlement to means-tested benefits such as Universal Credit, and a large win must be reported. This is the tax-adjacent consequence people most often miss.

Divorce and financial settlements

A win is an asset and forms part of the matrimonial pot in the ordinary way.

The four exceptions in detail

1. Interest and investment returns

The win is tax-free. The moment it sits in a savings account, any interest is savings income taxable in the normal way, subject to your Personal Savings Allowance (£1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate, nil for additional-rate). A £500,000 win generating £20,000 of interest creates a real tax liability on that interest.

2. Cryptoasset gains

Covered above. Capital Gains Tax on appreciation between acquisition and disposal, not on the win.

3. Inheritance and gifting

A large win becomes part of your estate and is subject to Inheritance Tax on death in the usual way, above the nil-rate band. Giving money away during your lifetime engages the seven-year rule for potentially exempt transfers. This catches many lottery and jackpot winners who assume "tax-free" means permanently outside the tax system — it does not; it means the win itself is not taxed at the point you receive it.

4. Gambling-adjacent income

Tipping services, streaming, affiliate commission, sponsorship and coaching are all trading income. So, incidentally, is the revenue that funds this website — see our about page.

Should you keep records?

You have no legal obligation to keep gambling records for tax purposes, because there is nothing to declare. Keep them anyway, for three practical reasons:

  • Your bank may query a large incoming payment under anti-money-laundering rules. A withdrawal confirmation and transaction history resolves it in minutes.
  • Cryptoasset disposals do require records if you use crypto casinos — dates, quantities and sterling values.
  • Knowing your actual position. Almost everybody who does not keep records believes they are further ahead than they are. This is the most valuable reason on the list, and it has nothing to do with HMRC.

Gambling tax FAQs

Do you pay tax on gambling winnings in the UK?

No. Gambling winnings are not taxable income in the United Kingdom. Betting duty on punters was abolished in 2001 and replaced with duties paid by operators — General Betting Duty at 15% and Remote Gaming Duty at 21% of gross profits. There is no threshold and nothing to declare, whether you win £50 or £5 million.

Do I have to declare gambling winnings to HMRC?

No. There is no declaration, no self-assessment entry and no threshold, because gambling winnings are not chargeable to income tax. The corresponding rule is that gambling losses are not deductible either. You may need to explain a large incoming payment to your bank under anti-money-laundering procedures, but that is not a tax matter.

Are winnings from offshore casinos taxed differently?

No. The tax treatment follows you rather than the operator's licence, because the exemption comes from how UK law characterises gambling rather than from where the casino is based. A win at a Curaçao-licensed site is as tax-free as one at a UK Gambling Commission licensed site.

Do professional gamblers pay tax in the UK?

Not on gambling winnings. The principle from Graham v Green (1925) is that gambling is not a trade, so its profits are not trading income, regardless of skill, scale or regularity. Income from activities around gambling is taxable, however — selling tips, streaming, sponsorship, affiliate commission and coaching are all trading income.

Do I pay tax on crypto casino winnings?

The gambling win itself is not taxable. But cryptoassets are property for Capital Gains Tax purposes, so if the sterling value of your crypto rises between acquiring it and selling it, that gain may be chargeable against your annual exempt amount. Using a dollar-pegged stablecoin such as USDT largely removes the issue. Keep a record of dates, quantities and sterling values.

Is a lottery win taxed in the UK?

No. National Lottery, EuroMillions and scratchcard prizes are tax-free in full at the point you receive them. What follows can be taxable: interest earned on the money is savings income, the sum forms part of your estate for Inheritance Tax, and gifts to family engage the seven-year rule for potentially exempt transfers.

Can I deduct gambling losses from my tax bill?

No, and this is the flip side of winnings being untaxed. Because UK law does not treat gambling as a trade, losses are not an allowable deduction against any other income. The symmetry is deliberate: taxing gains would invite the majority of gamblers, who lose, to claim relief.

Do gambling winnings affect benefits like Universal Credit?

They can. Gambling winnings are not taxable income, but they are capital, and capital above the relevant thresholds affects entitlement to means-tested benefits including Universal Credit. A significant win must be reported to the DWP. This is the consequence people most often overlook, precisely because the money is tax-free — the two questions are entirely separate.

Is spread betting taxed in the UK?

Financial spread betting is treated as gambling and its profits are not taxable. Contracts for difference are not gambling and do fall within Capital Gains Tax, despite feeling similar to a spread bet. The distinction is in the legal character of the instrument rather than how it behaves, and it catches people out regularly. If you trade both, keep them clearly separated in your records.

Are esports prize winnings taxed?

It depends which side of the screen you are on. Betting on esports is gambling and untaxed like any other bet. Prize money won by competing in a tournament is not gambling — it is income earned from an activity, and it is taxable, along with sponsorship, streaming revenue and appearance fees. The same distinction applies to poker: winning a tournament is untaxed, being paid to appear at one is not.

Do I pay tax on matched betting profits?

No. Matched betting profits arise from bookmaker free bets and bonuses and are treated as gambling winnings, so they are not taxable income. If you sell a matched betting service, run a subscription site or earn affiliate commission from it, that revenue is trading income and is taxable in the normal way.
